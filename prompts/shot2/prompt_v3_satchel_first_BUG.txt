SCENE CONTEXT
Seconds after the temple began to shake. The young explorer, still dry, bolts out of the ruins onto a narrow, vine-covered stone ledge along a jungle cliff and sprints toward the edge of a huge waterfall, the small gold idol in his right hand. On the run he rips the satchel off over his head and flings it away with his left hand, then snatches off his hat and flings it away too, to be light for the jump. He does not slow down: he leaps off the end of the ledge into the roaring white water. Seven seconds, ONE continuous take with no cuts, no dialogue.

FORMAT MODE
ONE continuous take, no cuts, no edits, no jump, no change of angle. A single unbroken camera move. First part, about 2.5 seconds: the camera glides smoothly behind and slightly above him as he sprints along the ledge at full speed, steady, no shake. Middle part, about 1.5 seconds, the same take, ONE flowing choreographed sequence timed to his stride: on one stride his LEFT hand hooks the satchel strap and lifts it clean over his head; on the next stride the same arm sweeps forward and out and the satchel is already flying off the ledge into the mist; the arm continues in the same sweeping arc, back across his body, snatches the hat off his head, and flings it spinning off the other side into the mist; on the very next stride he plants his foot at the broken end of the ledge and launches. Every motion flows into the next without a pause, as if he has rehearsed this exact sequence a thousand times. The idol stays tight in his RIGHT hand throughout. Final part, about 3 seconds, the same take: he is airborne, the camera keeps moving, rises and pulls back and out into a wide shot, following his arc out over the drop toward the white curtain of water. The take ends as he drops into the white water and spray and disappears into it.

PERFORMANCE
Total commitment. He runs with his head down and arms pumping, the idol held tight in his right hand. At the last step his jaw is set, a flash of wild nerve, no hesitation. Every movement is practised, economical and exact, as if rehearsed a thousand times: no fumbling, no hesitation, no look down at his hands, his eyes fixed on the end of the ledge. The satchel throw, the hat throw and the leap happen in a split second and flow into one another like a single movement of one dance. In the air his body stretches out. No speaking.

PHYSICS
Wet stone under his boots, small splashes of mist from each step, loose pebbles kicked off the ledge. His jacket whips behind him. The strap clears his head without snagging and the hat comes off without catching. His running speed does not drop at any point. The satchel and the hat each sail out in a clean arc and fall into the mist below; the hat spins as it goes. He takes off from the very end of the ledge with one driving leg. His arc is a real jump, not a flight: he rises a little, then falls toward the water. Spray from the waterfall drifts across the frame. He stays DRY until the moment he hits the water.

AUDIO
