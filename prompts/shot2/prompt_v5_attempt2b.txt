SCENE CONTEXT
Seconds after the temple began to shake. The young explorer, still dry, bolts out of the ruins onto a narrow, vine-covered stone ledge along a jungle cliff and sprints toward the edge of a huge waterfall, the small gold idol in his right hand. On the run he first snatches off his hat with his left hand and flings it away. Then he lifts the strap of the satchel up and over his bare head with the same left hand, and dumps the satchel off the ledge, to be light for the jump. He does not slow down: he leaps off the end of the ledge into the roaring white water. Eight seconds, ONE continuous take with no cuts, no dialogue.

FORMAT MODE
ONE continuous take, no cuts, no edits, no jump, no change of angle. A single unbroken camera move. First part, about 2 seconds: the camera glides smoothly behind and slightly above him as he sprints along the ledge at full speed, steady, no shake. Middle part, about 2.5 seconds, the same take: a fluid rehearsed sequence, timed to his stride, in this EXACT ORDER. STEP 1, the hat: his LEFT hand reaches up, snatches the hat off his head and flings it spinning out to the right over the edge into the mist. STEP 2, the strap: the same LEFT hand comes down and hooks the satchel strap at his right shoulder, up near his neck. He ducks his head slightly forward and lifts the strap up and OVER HIS HEAD, clearly visible, the strap passing over his head and clearing it completely, so the strap is now off his body and hangs from his left hand with the satchel swinging below. STEP 3, the dump: the left arm sweeps forward and out and releases, and the satchel sails off the ledge into the mist. The satchel can only leave his body AFTER the strap has gone over his head. STEP 4, the leap: on the very next stride he plants his foot at the broken end of the ledge and launches. Every motion flows into the next without a pause, as if he has rehearsed it a thousand times. The idol stays tight in his RIGHT hand throughout. Final part, about 3.5 seconds, the same take: he is airborne, the camera keeps moving, rises and pulls back and out into a wide shot, following his arc out over the drop toward the white curtain of water. The take ends as he drops into the white water and spray and disappears into it.

PERFORMANCE
Total commitment. He runs with his head down and arms pumping, the idol held tight in his right hand. At the last step his jaw is set, a flash of wild nerve, no hesitation. Every movement is practised, economical and exact, as if rehearsed a thousand times: no fumbling, no hesitation, no look down at his hands, his eyes fixed on the end of the ledge. The hat throw, the strap lift, the satchel dump and the leap flow into one another like a single movement of one dance. In the air his body stretches out. No speaking.

The strap clears his head without snagging. His running speed does not drop at any point. The satchel and the hat each sail out in a clean arc and fall into the mist below; the hat spins as it goes. He takes off from the very end of the ledge with one driving leg. His arc is a real jump, not a flight: he rises a little, then falls toward the water. Spray from the waterfall drifts across the frame. He stays DRY until the moment he hits the water.
